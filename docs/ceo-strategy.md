### Precision Pesticide Intelligence via Satellite AI

## Executive Summary

AgroLens is a B2B SaaS company that uses freely available Sentinel-2 satellite imagery combined with proprietary AI/ML models to generate per-field Variable Rate Application (VRA) prescription maps — telling farmers exactly where to apply more or less pesticide, season by season, field by field. Our core thesis: open satellite data dramatically lowers our input costs versus competitors who pay for commercial imagery, pure-play focus on pesticide reduction creates a defensible regulatory moat as the EU's Farm to Fork targets and US EPA ESA strategies tighten, and the $88B global crop protection chemicals market creates enormous willingness-to-pay among any customer who can demonstrably reduce their input bill.

The opportunity is large, the regulatory tailwinds are real, the incumbent tools are expensive and unfocused, and the core technology stack is achievable with a lean team. We target profitability at Month 30 with 120,000 subscribed hectares under management.

### 1.4 Regulatory Tailwinds

**EU Farm to Fork Strategy**
The EU's Farm to Fork Strategy, embedded in the European Green Deal, set a target of a **50% reduction in the use and risk of chemical pesticides by 2030** and a 50% reduction in use of more hazardous pesticides. While the proposed Sustainable Use Regulation (SUR) was withdrawn by the European Commission in February 2024 after opposition in the EU Parliament, individual EU Member States remain committed to national reduction plans that collectively must achieve the EU-wide target. This creates persistent demand for tools that help farmers document, reduce, and report pesticide usage — exactly what AgroLens provides.

**EU Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) 2023–2027**
The CAP's eco-scheme payments directly incentivize farmers to adopt precision application techniques. AgroLens-generated VRA maps can serve as compliance documentation for eco-scheme payments, creating a direct financial link between our platform and EU subsidies.

**US EPA Endangered Species Act (ESA) Pesticide Strategies**
The US EPA finalized its Herbicide Strategy in August 2024 and its Insecticide Strategy in April 2025, establishing mitigation requirements for pesticide use near endangered species habitats. The EPA expects its FIFRA-related ESA consultations to extend into the 2030s. Farmers in affected zones face increasing compliance requirements — AgroLens VRA maps that demonstrate precision, targeted application provide a defensible compliance record.

**Carbon and Biodiversity Markets**
The voluntary agriculture carbon credit market was valued at **$36.1 million in 2024** but is projected to grow to **$648 million by 2034** (CAGR 31.9%, GMInsights). Reduced pesticide use is a co-benefit creditable under several regenerative agriculture credit methodologies. USDA announced **$7.7 billion** in climate-smart agriculture funding for FY2025. AgroLens data can feed directly into carbon MRV (measurement, reporting, and verification) workflows, creating a second revenue stream or a reason for government agencies to be anchor customers.

## 3. Product Vision

### 3.1 Core Product Concept

AgroLens is a **satellite-powered precision pesticide intelligence platform**. The fundamental value exchange: farmers upload their field boundaries; we ingest freely available Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery (10m resolution, 5-day revisit over Europe) plus optionally Planet Labs 3m daily imagery; our AI/ML pipeline produces actionable **Variable Rate Application (VRA) prescription maps** showing zone-level pesticide application recommendations. These maps are exported in formats compatible with all major variable rate spraying equipment (ISOBUS/ISO 11783, Deere GS3, CNH AFS) and displayed in a farmer-facing web dashboard and mobile app.

The moat: pure-play pesticide focus (not a generalist farm management tool), open Sentinel-2 data eliminating per-image data costs, and EU regulatory compliance workflows built in from day one.

### 3.2 MVP Feature Set (Months 0–6)

The MVP proves the core value proposition with a minimal, technically sound implementation targeting the first 10 pilot farms.

**Data Ingestion**
- Field boundary upload via GeoJSON, KML, or manual polygon drawing on map
- Automatic Sentinel-2 imagery retrieval via ESA Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem API (free) for uploaded fields
- Cloud masking and compositing to produce clean imagery tiles per field per 10–14 day window

**AI/ML Analysis Engine**
- NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) computation to identify crop stress zones within each field
- NDRE (Normalized Difference Red Edge Index) for crop health differentiation
- Simple zone delineation algorithm: divide each field into 3 management zones (low/medium/high application intensity) based on vegetation index clustering
- Baseline crop stress anomaly detection: flag fields with unusual stress compared to regional average

**VRA Prescription Map Generation**
- Per-field VRA map as a visual heatmap in the dashboard (3-zone color-coded map)
- Export to ISOBUS-compatible shapefile (.shp) for direct upload to variable rate controllers
- PDF prescription report for agronomists without connected equipment

**Dashboard**
- Web-based dashboard (responsive, works on tablet in field)
- Field overview map with health status indicators
- Time-series view of NDVI trend per field (last 12 months)
- Season-over-season comparison
- Pesticide usage log (manual entry by farmer, used for ROI tracking)
- Simple alert system: email/SMS when field stress crosses threshold

**Account & Billing**
- Self-service account creation and field boundary upload
- Stripe-integrated subscription billing (monthly or annual)
- Starter plan (up to 100 ha): €49/month
- Farmer plan (up to 500 ha): €199/month
- Pro plan (up to 2,000 ha): €599/month

### 3.3 Version 1 — Full Product (Months 7–12)

- Integration of Planet Labs 3m daily imagery as premium add-on for higher resolution zones
- Disease and pest risk modeling: overlay regional weather data (ERA5, open-access) with vegetation anomalies to predict fungal disease risk windows (e.g., septoria in wheat)
- Weed pressure mapping: spectral signature analysis for weed hotspot detection within fields
- Crop-specific models: trained models for wheat, rapeseed, maize, soybeans
- Agronomist portal: multi-farm management dashboard for advisors managing 20+ farms
- API (REST): programmatic access for integration with farm management systems (FMS) and agri-input retailer platforms
- Basic carbon/sustainability report: auto-generated annual report showing pesticide reduction vs. previous year or regional benchmark, exportable as PDF for CAP eco-scheme documentation
- Mobile app (iOS and Android): field-level notifications, offline VRA map viewing

### 3.4 Version 2 — Platform Scale (Months 13–24)

- Hyperspectral and SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) data fusion for cloud-penetrating crop monitoring
- Yield prediction model: pre-harvest yield estimation by zone, enabling input cost optimization earlier in the season
- Integrated market connectivity: link VRA recommendations to retailer ordering systems (order reduced inputs directly from dashboard)
- ML model marketplace: allow third-party agronomic models to run on AgroLens field data (revenue share)
- Full carbon MRV module: generate audit-ready pesticide reduction certificates for voluntary carbon markets and CAP eco-scheme claims
- Enterprise multi-tenant architecture: white-label deployment for large agri-retailers and cooperatives
- Predictive resistance management: flag fields at risk of developing herbicide resistance based on application history patterns

## 7. Key Risks & Mitigations

### Risk 1: Sentinel-2 Cloud Cover Reduces Data Quality in Key Periods

**Risk Description:** Sentinel-2 is an optical satellite — cloud cover prevents imagery acquisition. Northwestern Europe (primary target market: France, Germany, Netherlands) experiences significant cloud cover in spring and autumn, precisely when pesticide application decisions are most time-critical. If we cannot deliver timely, cloud-free imagery, VRA maps are outdated and farmers lose confidence in the platform.

**Likelihood:** High. Northwestern Europe averages 50–60% cloud cover on any given day.

**Mitigation:**
1. Implement multi-temporal compositing: combine imagery from multiple overpasses over a 10–14 day window to produce cloud-free composites. This reduces temporal resolution but ensures coverage.
2. Integrate Sentinel-1 SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) data — also free via Copernicus — as a cloud-penetrating complement. SAR provides soil moisture and crop biomass proxies even through 100% cloud cover.
3. For premium-tier customers (Pro/Enterprise), offer optional Planet Labs 3m daily imagery as a cloud-gap-fill supplement at a modest price premium.
4. Communicate clearly to farmers: VRA maps are updated on a compositing window schedule (every 10–14 days), not daily. Manage expectations proactively.

---

### Risk 2: Farmer Technology Adoption Resistance

**Risk Description:** McKinsey's 2024 Voice of the Global Farmer survey confirmed that 49% of European farmers cite implementation costs as prohibitive for precision ag adoption. Even at our low price point, the behavioral change of uploading field boundaries and downloading VRA maps — then calibrating variable rate sprayers — creates friction. Early adopter farmers are not representative; mainstream adoption may stall.

**Likelihood:** Medium-High.

**Mitigation:**
1. Make onboarding radically simple: the entire process from account creation to first VRA map must be achievable in under 20 minutes with no technical skills required.
2. Partner with agronomists and crop advisors rather than selling only direct to farmers. Agronomists are trusted intermediaries who handle the technical setup on behalf of farmers.
3. Offer a free tier (up to 50 ha) for the first season so farmers can evaluate the product with zero financial risk before converting to paid.
4. Provide hands-on onboarding support (video call or on-farm visit) for the first 50 customers to gather qualitative feedback and reduce early churn.

### Risk 3: EU Regulatory Uncertainty Following SUR Withdrawal

**Risk Description:** The EU Sustainable Use Regulation — the primary binding legislative instrument for Farm to Fork pesticide targets — was withdrawn by the European Commission in February 2024. Without binding legislation, the urgency of regulatory-driven demand for pesticide reduction tools may be lower than anticipated, weakening a core element of our go-to-market narrative.

**Likelihood:** Medium.

**Mitigation:**
1. Reframe the narrative from "regulatory compliance" to "direct cost savings." Even without binding EU law, input cost inflation makes pesticide reduction economically compelling independent of regulation. Our primary value proposition is saving €20–50/ha in input costs, not compliance.
2. Focus on CAP eco-scheme documentation as a near-term, real incentive. CAP eco-scheme payments are actively paying farmers now and require documented sustainable practice records — AgroLens provides these without requiring new legislation.
3. Monitor the SUR and related national legislation closely. Multiple EU member states (Germany, France) are advancing their own national pesticide reduction action plans regardless of EU-level legislation. Be positioned to respond rapidly when the regulatory environment clarifies.
4. The EU is actively working on a new approach to pesticide reduction through the "Integrated Pest Management" framework — maintain relationships with DG AGRI and relevant NGOs to stay ahead of regulatory developments.

### Risk 4: Competition from Well-Funded Incumbents Launching Pesticide VRA Features

**Risk Description:** Climate FieldView (250M+ subscribed acres), Taranis ($100M+ funded), and EOSDA could each add pesticide-specific VRA map generation as a feature within 6–12 months, using their existing satellite data infrastructure and customer relationships to undercut our positioning.

**Likelihood:** Medium.

**Mitigation:**
1. Move fast to establish customer lock-in through agronomist partnerships and cooperative distribution agreements. Switching costs increase significantly once a farmer has uploaded field boundaries, calibrated VRA outputs to their specific machinery, and trained their agronomist on the platform.
2. Build differentiation that is structurally hard for incumbents to replicate: (a) full independence from input sellers (FieldView/Bayer can't credibly do this), (b) EU CAP eco-scheme compliance as a first-class feature (US-centric incumbents won't prioritize EU regulatory nuance), (c) open-source model transparency (we publish our NDVI-to-prescription methodology, enabling farmer and agronomist trust — incumbents keep models proprietary).
3. Focus aggressively on network effects within cooperatives: once a cooperative recommends AgroLens to its members and uses it for benchmarking across its member farms, individual farmer adoption becomes a social norm within that cooperative community.

### Risk 5: AI/ML Model Accuracy Below Farmer Expectations Leading to Crop Damage or Loss

**Risk Description:** VRA prescription maps that are systematically wrong — e.g., recommending too-low pesticide application in a zone that then develops a serious disease outbreak — could result in significant crop losses for farmers. In severe cases, this creates liability exposure, reputational damage, and churn. A single high-profile crop failure attributed to AgroLens recommendations could be existential for the company in its early stages.

**Likelihood:** Low-Medium (Sentinel-2 at 10m resolution has limitations for within-field fine-scale variation).

**Mitigation:**
1. Frame all VRA maps as "decision support" not "automated instructions." All outputs include clear confidence scores and a disclaimer that recommendations should be reviewed by a qualified agronomist before application.
2. Build explicit uncertainty quantification into the AI model: when confidence in zone classification is below a threshold (e.g., insufficient cloud-free imagery in the compositing window), the system defaults to recommending standard uniform application rather than a variable rate prescription.
3. Carry appropriate professional indemnity and product liability insurance from Day 1.
4. In the first two seasons (MVP and V1), actively involve agronomists in reviewing all VRA maps before delivery to farmers. Use this human-in-the-loop period to validate model accuracy and build a labeled ground-truth dataset that improves model performance.
5. Set initial pilot farms with experienced, tech-savvy farmers who understand that the platform is in early validation — be fully transparent about the confidence and limitations of early-stage AI models.

## 8. What the Planner Needs to Build — MVP Feature Requirements

The following is a prioritized, requirements-level feature list for the MVP (Months 0–6). Items are listed in implementation priority order. All features must be production-quality, secure, and scalable to 10,000 users within 12 months from launch without architectural refactoring.

### Priority 1 — Core Data Pipeline (Must-have, Weeks 1–8)

**REQ-01: Field Boundary Management**
- Users can create and manage fields via a web interface
- Fields defined by uploading a GeoJSON or KML file, or by drawing a polygon on an interactive map (Leaflet.js or Mapbox GL JS)
- Each field stores: name, crop type (dropdown: wheat, rapeseed, barley, maize, soybeans), planting date, and geometry
- Field area computed automatically from geometry (hectares, displayed to 1 decimal place)
- Maximum 50 fields per Starter account; 200 per Farmer; unlimited per Pro/Enterprise
- All field geometries stored in PostGIS-enabled PostgreSQL database

**REQ-02: Sentinel-2 Imagery Retrieval**
- Automated retrieval of Sentinel-2 L2A (atmospherically corrected) imagery for all user fields via the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem STAC API (free, no per-image cost)
- Retrieval triggered automatically when new scenes are available (check every 24 hours via cron job)
- Scene selection criteria: (a) overlaps field geometry, (b) cloud cover < 80% in the scene tile, (c) acquired within the last 30 days
- Download bands: B02 (Blue), B03 (Green), B04 (Red), B05 (Red Edge), B08 (NIR), B11 (SWIR) — minimum required for NDVI, NDRE, and moisture indices
- Imagery stored in cloud-optimized GeoTIFF (COG) format in S3-compatible object storage; original scenes not retained after index computation (cost reduction)
- Processing and storage must not exceed €0.20/ha/year total infrastructure cost

**REQ-03: Vegetation Index Computation**
- Compute per-pixel NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red) for each cloud-masked scene
- Compute per-pixel NDRE = (Red Edge - Red) / (Red Edge + Red) for each scene
- Apply cloud and shadow masking using the Scene Classification Layer (SCL) band included in Sentinel-2 L2A products
- Generate 10-day composites by taking the median pixel value across all valid (non-cloud) observations in the window
- Store computed index rasters per field per compositing window in COG format

**REQ-04: Management Zone Delineation**
- Apply k-means clustering (k=3) to the combined NDVI and NDRE composite rasters for each field
- Output: 3 zone polygons per field labeled "Low", "Medium", "High" (corresponding to crop stress / application intensity)
- Zone polygons stored as GeoJSON in database, linked to the compositing window date
- Minimum zone size: 0.5 ha (merge smaller zones into adjacent dominant zone to avoid impractical micro-zones)
- Zone delineation must complete within 60 seconds per field on standard cloud compute (c5.large or equivalent)

**REQ-05: VRA Prescription Map Generation**
- Prescription engine translates zone labels into application rate multipliers based on crop type and application type:
  - Fungicide: Low zone = 0.6x base rate, Medium = 1.0x, High = 1.3x
  - Herbicide: Low zone = 0.7x base rate, Medium = 1.0x, High = 1.2x
  - Insecticide: Low zone = 0.5x base rate, Medium = 1.0x, High = 1.5x
- User inputs base application rate (L/ha) and application type to generate final prescription
- Prescription stored as zone polygon GeoJSON with rate attribute
- API endpoint: `POST /api/v1/fields/{fieldId}/prescriptions` returns prescription GeoJSON

**REQ-06: Shapefile Export (ISOBUS-compatible)**
- Export prescription as ESRI Shapefile (.shp + .dbf + .prj + .shx) in WGS84 projection
- Shapefile attribute table: ZoneID, RateL_ha (application rate in L/ha), RatePct (percentage of base rate)
- Export available via dashboard button and `GET /api/v1/fields/{fieldId}/prescriptions/{id}/export?format=shp` API endpoint
- Additional export formats: GeoJSON, PDF report (PDF must include: farm name, field name, date, crop, application type, zone map image, zone area breakdown table, estimated input savings vs. uniform rate)

### Priority 2 — Web Dashboard (Must-have, Weeks 5–12)

**REQ-07: Farmer Dashboard — Field Overview**
- Authenticated web app (Next.js or similar React framework, server-side rendered for SEO)
- Landing page: map-based overview of all user fields color-coded by current health status (Green: normal, Amber: mild stress, Red: significant stress)
- Field health status derived from NDVI deviation from 30-day rolling average for that field
- Clicking a field opens the field detail view

**REQ-08: Field Detail View**
- NDVI time-series chart: 12-month rolling chart of average field NDVI (line chart, rendered with Chart.js or Recharts)
- Current VRA map: renders the most recent prescription zone map as a colored overlay on a satellite basemap
- Zone breakdown table: area (ha) and recommended rate for each zone
- Export button: downloads shapefile and PDF report
- Imagery date displayed: "Based on imagery: [date range of composite]"
- Alert if most recent composite is older than 21 days due to cloud cover — suggest fall-back to uniform rate

**REQ-09: Notifications**
- Email notification when new VRA map is available for any user field (templated email, sent via SendGrid or equivalent)
- Email alert when field NDVI drops more than 15% below 30-day rolling average (potential disease/pest event)
- User configurable: can enable/disable notifications per field in account settings

**REQ-10: Mobile-Responsive Design**
- All dashboard views must be fully usable on a 10-inch tablet (landscape) in the field
- VRA map must render offline after initial load (service worker caches the map tile for the currently selected field)
- Offline mode must display last-downloaded VRA map with a clear "Last updated: [date]" indicator

### Priority 3 — Account, Billing & API (Must-have, Weeks 8–14)

**REQ-11: Authentication & Account Management**
- Email + password authentication with email verification
- OAuth login via Google (optional but strongly preferred for ease of onboarding)
- Password reset flow
- Account settings: name, email, farm name, country, phone number
- GDPR-compliant data deletion: user can request full account and field data deletion via settings page

**REQ-12: Subscription Billing**
- Stripe Checkout integration for subscription management
- Three plan tiers: Starter (€49/mo or €470/yr), Farmer (€199/mo or €1,910/yr), Pro (€599/mo or €5,750/yr)
- Annual billing encouraged with a "Save 20%" badge
- Webhook handling for Stripe events: subscription created, subscription cancelled, payment failed
- Automatic account downgrade / access restriction on failed payment (grace period: 7 days)
- Usage metering: display current hectare count vs. plan limit in account dashboard; warning at 80% of limit

**REQ-13: REST API (MVP scope)**
- Base URL: `https://api.agrolens.io/v1/`
- Authentication: Bearer token (JWT), token generated in account settings
- Endpoints required for MVP:
  - `GET /fields` — list all fields for authenticated user
  - `POST /fields` — create field (accepts GeoJSON geometry + metadata)
  - `GET /fields/{id}/vra-maps` — list available VRA maps for field with dates
  - `GET /fields/{id}/vra-maps/{mapId}` — get VRA map GeoJSON
  - `GET /fields/{id}/vra-maps/{mapId}/export` — download shapefile (returns presigned S3 URL)
  - `GET /fields/{id}/ndvi-timeseries` — get NDVI time-series data (array of {date, ndvi_mean})
- API rate limit: 100 requests/minute per API key
- OpenAPI 3.0 specification published at `https://api.agrolens.io/v1/openapi.json`

### Priority 4 — Infrastructure & Operations (Must-have before launch)

**REQ-14: Infrastructure Architecture**
- Cloud platform: AWS (preferred) or GCP
- Application: containerized (Docker), deployed via Kubernetes (EKS or GKE) or a managed equivalent (AWS Fargate)
- Database: PostgreSQL 15+ with PostGIS extension (AWS RDS or Cloud SQL)
- Object storage: S3 (or GCS) for imagery COGs and exported files
- Data pipeline: Apache Airflow or AWS Step Functions for orchestrating daily imagery retrieval and processing jobs
- CI/CD: GitHub Actions pipeline with automated testing, linting, and staging deployment on pull request merge
- Environments: Development, Staging, Production — all isolated

**REQ-15: Security & Compliance**
- All data encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3)
- GDPR Article 30 processing records maintained
- Data residency: all EU customer data stored in EU-region cloud data centers (e.g., AWS eu-west-1, eu-central-1)
- SOC 2 Type I audit initiated by Month 12 (not required for MVP but architecture must support it)
- No raw Sentinel-2 scene data retained after index computation to minimize storage costs and data liability

**REQ-16: Monitoring & Alerting**
- Application performance monitoring: Datadog or equivalent; alert on p95 latency > 2s for any API endpoint
- Pipeline monitoring: alert on-call if any field fails to receive a new composite within 21 days
- Uptime target: 99.5% for the web application (MVP), 99.9% target by Month 12
- Error tracking: Sentry for frontend and backend error capture

### Out of Scope for MVP (Deferred to V1)

The following features are explicitly deferred to Version 1 (Months 7–12) to maintain MVP focus:
- Planet Labs imagery integration
- Disease risk prediction model
- Weed pressure mapping
- Mobile native app (iOS/Android)
- Agronomist multi-farm portal
- Carbon/sustainability report module
- Multi-language localization (MVP delivers in English; French and German in V1)
